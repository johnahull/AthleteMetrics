## Summary
Varsity Boys tested 24 athletes in the Vertical Jump and the 10-Yard Fly. Jumping is the stronger area: 58% of athletes meet the Varsity benchmark (14/24). Speed is the main gap, with 13 of 24 athletes in the Developing tier of Speed Tiers. This report has no history over time, so it shows where the team stands today, not how it is changing.

## What's Going Well
- **Vertical Jump**: the team average is 24.60 in and the median is 24.50 in, and 14/24 athletes meet the Varsity benchmark.
- **Top-end speed**: 3 athletes reached the Elite tier and 8 reached the Advanced tier of Speed Tiers in the 10-Yard Fly, so the group has a fast core to build around.

## What to Work On
- **10-Yard Fly**: 54% of athletes sit in the bottom tier (Developing) of Speed Tiers. The team average is 1.32 s, with results from 1.18 s to 1.51 s.
- **Vertical Jump spread**: results run from 18.00 in to 31.50 in (SD 3.10), so a large part of the roster still sits below the Varsity benchmark.

## Next Steps
- **10-Yard Fly gap**: use the two weekly sprint sessions for flying sprints and max-velocity work, with the goal of moving athletes out of the Developing tier.
- **Vertical Jump gap**: add short jump and landing blocks for the athletes below the Varsity benchmark, kept separate from heavy sprint days.
- **Build history**: retest both metrics later in the season so the next report can show change over time instead of a single snapshot.
