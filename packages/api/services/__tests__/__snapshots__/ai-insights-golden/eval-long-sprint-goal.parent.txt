## What the metrics mean (vetted)
Vetted sport meaning; use it for sport-meaning claims and add nothing of your own.
- 10-Yard Fly (30 yd run-in): How fast the athlete moves once already running; the running start is the distance run before timing starts. Short running starts are still speeding up, long ones are near top speed. Compare only times with the same running start. Matters most in longer sprints.
- 10-Yard Dash: How fast the athlete gets going from a standstill. A slow time means the first steps have room to grow. Every sprint starts here, and a slow start costs time in every sprint, including a long one.
- 20-Yard Dash: How well speed keeps building after the first steps. Covers most short game sprints; with the start, it shows how much of a race goes to speeding up.
- 40-Yard Dash: Overall straight-line speed. A slow time can come from the start, from top speed, or both; the splits tell which. Read the splits before choosing what to train.
- 5-10-5 Agility: How quickly the athlete stops, turns and gets going again; a slow time can come from the turns or from straight speed. Change of direction in tight spaces; compare with the short straight sprint times to see what the turns cost.
- Countermovement Jump: How high the athlete jumps after a quick dip with no arm swing; leg power on its own. Supports jumping and the push of the first sprint steps; read it with the paused-squat jump. Compare only jumps done the same way.

- Write at a 6th-8th grade reading level. Keep sentences under 20 words.

WHAT RESULTS MEAN:
- These rules and NEVER SAY override the checklist examples below.
