## What the metrics mean (vetted)
Sport meaning of these results, vetted by the performance staff. Use it for sport-meaning claims; add nothing of your own.
- 10-Yard Fly (30 yd run-in): How fast the athlete moves once already running. Compare only times from the same running start; when it comes from one longer run, it is a piece of that run, not a separate sprint. Short running starts are mostly still speeding up; long ones are close to top speed. Top speed matters most in longer sprints.
- 10-Yard Dash: How fast the athlete gets going from a standstill. A slow time means the first steps have room to grow. Every sprint starts here; a quicker start lowers the top speed a long sprint goal needs.
- 20-Yard Dash: How well speed keeps building after the first steps. Covers most short game sprints; with the start, it shows how much of a race goes to speeding up.
- 40-Yard Dash: Overall straight-line speed. A slow time can come from the start, from top speed, or both; the splits tell which. Read the splits before choosing what to train.
- 5-10-5 Agility: How quickly the athlete stops, turns and gets going again; a slow time can come from the turns or from straight speed. Change of direction in tight spaces; compare with the short straight sprint times to see what the turns cost.
- Countermovement Jump: How high the athlete jumps after a quick dip with no arm swing; leg power on its own. Supports jumping and the push of the first sprint steps; read it with the paused-squat jump.
